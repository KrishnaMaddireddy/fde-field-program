# Decomposition drills

Set a 30-minute timer. Write as if you were talking to the interviewer: narrate, state assumptions, ask the clarifying questions you would ask (and write the answer you assume). Then stop, even if unfinished.

Your AI tutor scores each drill out of 100, 20 points per step. Drills get their own feedback and don't change your module score:

| Step | What earns full points |
| --- | --- |
| Clarify | Restated goal, 3+ sharp clarifying questions, explicit assumptions |
| Stakeholders and metrics | Who decides, who uses, who can block; one metric with a baseline you would go get |
| Inputs | Data sources with owner, freshness, likely quality problems, access path |
| Subproblems | 4–7 pieces, ordered by value × confidence ÷ effort, riskiest assumption first |
| Walking skeleton | Something real in 1–2 weeks, how you'd measure it, what comes next |

---

## Case A — The operating room that starts late

A 6-hospital health system says: *"Our ORs start late almost every morning. We want to use AI to fix scheduling."* First-case on-time starts are their board's top operational metric. They have an Epic EHR, a separate staffing system, and a lot of opinions.

## Case B — The water utility

A mid-size city water utility says: *"We want AI to predict which water mains will break so we can replace them before they fail."* They have 1,900 miles of pipe, a GIS system, 30 years of work orders (partly on paper), and a fixed capital budget.

## Case C — The insurer's inbox

A regional property insurer receives 4,000 claim-related emails a week with attachments (photos, PDFs, invoices). The COO says: *"Automate claims intake with AI."* Claims adjusters are unionized. Regulators require that a human decides every claim.
